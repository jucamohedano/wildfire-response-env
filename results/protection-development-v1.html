A warning is not the same as protection.

Versioned instructions, fallible shelter and resource-bound warning teams. Spanish v1 and the existing benchmark remain separate and replayable.

Implemented lifecycle

Issue → delivery → acknowledgement → attempted action → physical outcome.

  • Shelter, evacuate or release, with recipient sequence and an explicit replacement ID.
  • Old instructions remain in flight; central intent cannot instantly unsend them. After a newer receipt, stale older instructions cannot execute.
  • Acknowledgement precedes execution. An unable population has an attempt time, but no execution time. Moving groups cannot teleport home or stop through release.
  • In-person warning requires an actual on-scene crew, public clear-cell evidence and four minutes of service, without simultaneous suppression/reassignment.

Shelter is not immunity

A synthetic nearby-fire smoke proxy accumulates at a lower rate while sheltering. Direct fire still exposes people; prolonged proxy exposure can also exceed the threshold.

These units, rates, buildings, service areas and communication delays are exercise assumptions—not medical, structural, route or operational safety predictions.

The counterexample is retained

In protect-dev-002-v2, shelter-first exposes 150 simulated people and receives zero scalar reward despite completed triage/communication goals. Evacuation-first keeps people unexposed here but leaves fire unresolved and scores 0.75.

This demonstrates the gate and fallible mechanics—not a claim that evacuation is universally superior, either policy is optimal or the scenario is infeasible.

Data gate: four anchors are still not ready for historical joins
