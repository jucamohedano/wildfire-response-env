Coordinate a changing incident with delayed information and finite resources.

The agent manages fire trucks, ground crews and fixed-wing aircraft, exchanges information with response centres, and chooses shelter or evacuation orders for population cohorts.

It selects missions, safe approaches and destinations; the router resolves travel. Mobilization, travel, setup, work, return and replenishment remain separate simulated stages.

The reward function

80% population protection · 20% terrain preserved · independently checked.

People protected

Fraction of the initially unexposed population that remains unexposed.

Terrain preserved

Fraction of initially unburned, burnable cells that avoids new burning.

0.8 × P + 0.2 × B

Weights, not an observed safety rate. Inherited harm and burned area are excluded. The score is computed at day end; censored or abandoned runs cannot earn a successful day score.

✓ Report active fire, burned area and unresolved evacuations alongside the score.

A high score can coexist with active fire. Reward calibration remains open in issue #27. Simulated exposure is an exercise measure, not a mortality estimate.

Synthetic

Missing incident-matched logs are replaced by declared exercise inputs: roster, stocks, delays and setup/refill times. Duty windows are staffing periods; replenishment requires a compatible facility.

Supported height crop and masks are retained. Routes use mapped corridors and declared walking assumptions. Missing links are not invented; terrain metadata and field access need evidence.

Which variables change?

The active bank uses six designed, pinned day scenarios. The dimensions below vary by scenario design or seeded execution; they are not all independently resampled in every active case.

  • Fire configuration and event timingSynthetic ignition footprint, aged handover state and scheduled changes. The source-map wind case shifts the initial fire pattern; all initial fires must remain on source-known fuel and satisfy warm-up/boundary checks.
  • Wind and operational disruptionsWind direction/magnitude and change time; loss of vehicle approaches; aircraft-unavailable windows; destination closures; centre shortages. Conditions alter access and actions consistently.
  • Report and response latencyReaction and transmission delays are seeded separately by source, role, kind, channel, urgency and connectivity settings. Current profiles use bounded delays up to 60 minutes. Text follows event templates; it is not a historical transcript.
  • Resources and population stateScenario-defined readiness/holds, assignments, finite water, shifts and facilities. Initial destination occupancy changes in the relocation case. The current population remains two synthetic cohorts of 200 and 150; it is not broadly resampled.
Reproducibility is more than a seed. Scenario version + seed + input/network hashes identify a run. Current day IDs have pinned seeds and reject arbitrary overrides. Geography, the 24-hour horizon and the 80/20 reward stay fixed across this active bank.
What the archived larger sampler varied

The two-hour expansion sampled ignition offsets/times, five wind vectors, report delays, duplicate counts, resource holds, request delays, readback mismatch, broadcast delay/failure and wind-shift timing. It produced 960 configuration groups with two seeded/wording variants each.

Its design seed was 20261026; per-case seeds were 82000000 + 2 × group_index + variant. That sampler is archived: it had night-only horizons and retained intent warnings. These factors are documented history, not a released current day-mode training corpus. Original generator and source context ↗

For a future training corpus, create separately versioned configuration groups before policy rollouts, keep related layouts/variants in one split, validate access and event constraints, then run references and replay checks. Randomization produces counterfactual exercises; it does not create new historical incidents, expert decisions or demonstrated real-world effectiveness. Next corpus work: #18 ↗
