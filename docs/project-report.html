The project
Control-centre coordinationINCIDENT-DAY v2
Working CPU environmentSource-grounded synthetic exercises

Coordinate a changing incident with delayed information and finite resources.

The environment turns coordination into a testable task. The agent manages fire trucks, ground crews and fixed-wing aircraft, exchanges information with response centres, and chooses shelter or evacuation orders for population cohorts.

It selects missions, safe approaches and destinations; the router resolves travel. Mobilization, travel, setup, work, return and replenishment remain separate simulated stages.

The project began as a hackathon call-dispatch system. It is now a standalone environment for testing decision policies. A future policy service could connect a trained model back to an operational workflow.
Current scope: related development exercises on one source landscape. A representative day-mode training corpus and field calibration are still needed. There is no demonstrated trained-policy improvement yet.

How synthetic data is generated

Pin the landscape, construct a scenario, run its consequences and retain a verifiable trajectory.

Gaps: what we estimate, and what stays unresolved

Fuel classes
80 cells
Estimated

Land-cover-compatible source neighbours vote for a class, using inverse distance and up to eight neighbours. These are categorical votes, not averages of class IDs. Explicit fallbacks cover missing support; three urban centres use an assumed built/garden representation with a nonzero 0.35 fuel multiplier.

Land or water
27 centres
Unresolved

These 27 centres are part of the 80 estimated cells. Missing cover does not establish sea, land or a firebreak. The uncertainty remains marked and episodes are censored when the fire approaches these centres. Fuel estimates do not resolve land/water truth.

Fire-front weather
Model assumptions

Coastal station measurements provide context. Runtime wind is a synthetic vector in model units; its conversion from measured m/s and mountain-wind representativeness are uncalibrated. Incident-specific fuel-moisture calibration is also missing.

Dispatch and response
Synthetic

There are no complete historical readiness, dispatch and acknowledgment logs. Rosters, initial stocks, duty windows, setup/refill times, communication delays and population behavior are declared exercise inputs. Examples include five-minute ground setup and six-minute aircraft reload.

Terrain and access
Restricted support

Retain a supported elevation crop and its masks; missing height is not flattened locally. Routes use retained mapped corridors and declared walking assumptions. Missing road links are not invented. Extract-specific terrain metadata and field access still need evidence.

The 80 fuel estimates have low confidence. Neighbour agreement is not a probability of correctness or proof of accuracy on the missing cells. Estimation method and provenance ↗

Which variables change?

The active bank uses six designed, pinned day scenarios. The dimensions below vary by scenario design or seeded execution; they are not all independently resampled in every active case.

  • Fire configuration and event timingSynthetic ignition footprint, aged handover state and scheduled changes. The source-map wind case shifts the initial fire pattern; all initial fires must remain on source-known fuel and satisfy warm-up/boundary checks.
  • Wind and operational disruptionsWind direction/magnitude and change time; loss of vehicle approaches; aircraft-unavailable windows; destination closures; centre shortages. Conditions alter access and actions consistently.
  • Report and response latencyReaction and transmission delays are seeded separately by source, role, kind, channel, urgency and connectivity settings. Current profiles use bounded delays up to 60 minutes. Text follows event templates; it is not a historical transcript.
  • Resources and population stateScenario-defined readiness/holds, assignments, finite water, shifts and facilities. Initial destination occupancy changes in the relocation case. The current population remains two synthetic cohorts of 200 and 150; it is not broadly resampled.
Reproducibility is more than a seed. Scenario version + seed + input/network hashes identify a run. Current day IDs have pinned seeds and reject arbitrary overrides. Geography, the 24-hour horizon and the 80/20 reward stay fixed across this active bank.
What the archived larger sampler varied

The two-hour expansion sampled ignition offsets/times, five wind vectors, report delays, duplicate counts, resource holds, request delays, readback mismatch, broadcast delay/failure and wind-shift timing. It produced 960 configuration groups with two seeded/wording variants each.

Its design seed was 20261026; per-case seeds were 82000000 + 2 × group_index + variant. That sampler is archived: it had night-only horizons and retained intent warnings. These factors are documented history, not a released current day-mode training corpus. Original generator and source context ↗

For a future training corpus, create separately versioned configuration groups before policy rollouts, keep related layouts/variants in one split, validate access and event constraints, then run references and replay checks. Randomization produces counterfactual exercises; it does not create new historical incidents, expert decisions or demonstrated real-world effectiveness. Next corpus work: #18 ↗

What changed after the realism audit, and what remains open

Fix defaults and coverage before interpreting benchmark scores.

✓ Corrected active path

Six fixture exercises or six source-map v2 exercises are selected from exact input/network pins. These are related views of six families, not 12 independent historical fires. Unknown packs have no implicit active default.

Four older catalogs and 20 superseded day configurations are archived. Their files, seeds and scores remain available for explicit replay. Removing them from discovery prevents accidental reuse as current training data.

Evidence still needed

Operational and physical calibration of timing, wind coupling, suppression and population effects. Source fuel/land/water and terrain-metadata gaps remain documented; map geometry alone does not certify vehicle access.

A new grouped training corpus, held-out policy comparisons and evidence of learning improvement. Code/data redistribution rights still need resolution before public release.
