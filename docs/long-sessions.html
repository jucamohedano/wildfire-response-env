Research and proposal · 27 October 2026 · not a runtime change

Longer incidents, without thousands of model turns

Keep the two-hour initial-response benchmark. Add long sessions as a separate, versioned experiment; do not redefine existing tasks or compare scores across horizons.

What the references actually do

  • AirOps v1: a 3–6-hour disruption focus, but 10–22 hourly watches for the surrounding operational timeline. The disruption window is not the full decision horizon. Dataset card.
  • AirOps v2/v3: a network storm day starting at 04:00, with hourly desk watches; the v2 turn machine can stop when no flight remains open. The horizon is task/deadline-driven, not universally 24 hours. Pinned turn machine.
  • PortSim: v1 plans 1–3 weeks in one submission; live v2–v5 tasks cover one week. Wind/lines weeks use 4–10 news watches, with separate model-turn budgets. A long simulated horizon need not imply minute-by-minute model calls. Dataset card.

Recommended progression

  1. Preserve 2 hours. It tests early triage, communication and protection, not full extinction. Freeze its tasks, reward and leaderboards.
  2. Pilot 6 hours. Test delayed reinforcement, new calls, sustained protection and reassignment. From the source clock of 21:35 this ends around 03:35: still a night-response exercise.
  3. Then 12 hours. Cross dawn only after aircraft operations, changing weather, replenishment and crew-duty assumptions have acceptance tests. Starting at 21:35 reaches 09:35; the current 07:00 daylight gate is an assumption, not a validated solar/aviation model.
  4. Defer 24-hour/multi-day mode. Add only if a new operational objective needs it and fuel, rest, shelters, weather and map extent support it.

Implementation sequence

  1. Version the session contract. Replace hard-coded 120-minute gates in the new environment, reward verifier, prompts, generator, adapters and UI. Never monkey-patch benchmark v1 or merely raise its turn count.
  2. Separate physics from decisions. Keep one-minute physics and delivery ordering. Retain two-minute urgent decisions; propose bounded waits up to ten minutes in stable periods. Interrupt only at delivered evidence or public deadlines, never because hidden fire ignited. Every intervening tick and delivery still executes. Invalid actions cannot request an arbitrary long skip.
  3. Make the late period meaningful. Seed incidents and calls beyond minute 120, version event/report budgets, preserve escalation and closure capacity, and avoid silently exhausting the current 64-update cap. Do not stretch two-hour weather into six hours without a declared process.
  4. Persist one continuous world. Checkpoint RNGs, clock, fire, dose, resources, pending deliveries, evidence and action history. Use content-addressed maps plus chunked transitions. Resume must match an uninterrupted run; chunks do not reset fuel, exposure, missions or reward.
  5. Keep population dominant. Retain the 80/20 priorities but compute cumulative harm and unique burned area over the whole session. Two-hour diagnostics are not extra terminal rewards. Keep issue #27 open; test shelter/re-entry and delayed-harm exploits before freezing new rules.

CPU-first acceptance and decision gate

Use six training-only configuration groups spanning urgent and false reports, duplicates/conflicts, multiple fires and failed delivery. Compare passive, dispatcher and cooperative policies at 2 and 6 hours: at most 36 episodes, one worker, ten-minute initial wall-time cap. A cap stop is incomplete, not a pass. No paid inference or training is needed for this stage.

  • Every completed trace replays exactly and independently reproduces reward; checkpoint/resume and short-wait/long-wait action-free segments agree.
  • No future evidence leaks; late reports, offer expiry, acknowledgements and ongoing evacuation survive waits and chunk boundaries without duplication.
  • Fire hitting the domain boundary remains censored. If longer cases are routinely censored, acquire a larger continuous validated map—do not wrap, flatten unknown cells or turn the boundary into a firebreak.
  • Record memory, CPU time, bytes and model-decision count. Keep related seeds, layouts and all horizon variants in the same split. Hold validation/test outcomes back until the design and reward checks are frozen.

Adopt six hours only if the added period creates consequential, observable decisions at affordable cost. Otherwise keep two hours and describe its scope honestly. Longer traces alone are not a better benchmark.
